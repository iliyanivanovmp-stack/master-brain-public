# LinkedIn Writing Rules

Platform-specific writing rules for all LinkedIn content. Apply every time a LinkedIn post is drafted or reviewed.

---

## Formatting

- 1 idea per line. Most lines 3–10 words.
- Blank lines between beats only — not after every sentence.
- Use lists only for 3+ items: problems = hyphens (-), deliverables = arrows (→), steps = 1) 2) 3)
- No hashtags. No asterisks (*). No markdown formatting of any kind.
- Never use ALL CAPS for emphasis.
- No emoji bullets (🚀 ✨ 💡) as list markers — use -, →, or 1) only.
- No TL;DR, "In summary", or "Key takeaways" headers.
- No Unicode bold or italic characters.
- Length targets: short posts = 60–150 words. Long posts = 250–450 words. Nothing in between.

---

## Punctuation

- em-dash (—) is banned. Replace with a period, comma, colon, or parentheses.
- Use ... (three typed dots) not the ellipsis character (…).
- Straight quotes only. No curly or smart quotes.

---

## Tone & Voice

- Operator voice: experienced and grounded. Not a guru, not a coach.
- Address one person: use "you", never "people" or "one".
- Concrete over abstract: "saved 14 hours a week" beats "improved efficiency".
- Numbers go before the unit: "14 hours", "3 clients", "$480/year". Use numerals for 10+.
- Every post must include at least one specific number. If no real data, use a believable placeholder: "14 hours/week", "3 of our last 5 clients", "$480/year".
- Parentheses carry tone — use them for asides, not dashes.
- No bold, no italic. If a word needs emphasis, rewrite the sentence.
- Short sentences carry weight. Long sentences explain. Mix both.
- Casual lowercase after a full stop is intentional when it fits: "that's it."

---

## Structural Bans

- No negation pivot more than once per post: "It's not X. It's Y." — once maximum.
- No throat-clearing transitions: Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, That said, Importantly, Ultimately, Subsequently.
- No cringe transition questions: "The result?" "The catch?" "Why does this matter?"
- No symmetric paragraphs — vary paragraph length. Real posts are uneven.
- No one-sentence-per-paragraph for the entire post — mix lengths.

---

## Banned Openers

Never start a post with:
- "In today's world" / "In today's fast-paced world" / "In today's digital age"
- "Let's be honest" / "Here's the thing" / "Picture this"
- "I used to believe X. Then Y happened."
- "Most people think X. They're wrong." / "Most founders do X."
- "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
- "Here's a hard truth nobody wants to hear:"
- "I just saved my client $X. Here's how:"
- Any opener ending with a colon and one-line tease: "Here's the thing:"

---

## Banned Closers

Never end a post with:
- "Agree?" / "Thoughts?" / "What do you think? Drop a comment 👇"
- "Tag someone who needs this."
- "Save this for later." / "Bookmark this."
- "Like and follow for more." / "Share if you found this useful."
- "Hope this helps."

---

## Slop Scrub — Banned Phrases

Kill on sight: in today's fast-paced world, dive in, deep dive, delve into, navigate the landscape, navigate the complexities, unlock the power, unlock the potential, unleash your potential, at its core, it's important to note, game changer, game-changer, move the needle, stay ahead of the curve, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-gen, seamless, world-class, leverage (as verb), synergy, embark on a journey, foster a culture, drive impact, drive value, take a moment to, buckle up, here's the kicker, here's the catch, let that sink in, read that again, spoiler:, plot twist:, I'm thrilled to announce, I'm humbled to announce, couldn't be more proud, trust the process, your network is your net worth, soft skills are the new hard skills, hope this helps, optimize (as buzzword), empower (as buzzword), revolutionize, transformative, innovative (as buzzword), holistic, comprehensive (as buzzword), meticulous, unwavering, groundbreaking, robust (used loosely), scalable (used as buzzword), streamline, amplify, spearhead, underscore, resonate, illuminate, shed light, beacon, treasure trove, symphony, notably, arguably, indeed, pivotal, crucial (overused), vital (overused).

Single-word replacements: leverage→use, utilize→use, empower→help, robust→strong, seamless→smooth, optimize→improve, delve→look into, dive→go into, navigate→handle, foster→build, harness→use, elevate→raise, transform→change, revolutionize→change, holistic→full, comprehensive→full, pivotal→key, meticulous→careful, unwavering→steady, multifaceted→complex, intricate→complex, nuanced→subtle, synergy→(delete), paradigm→(plain word), ecosystem→space, landscape→space.

---

## Corrector Pass (always apply after drafting)

- Remove all em-dashes — replace with period, comma, colon, or parentheses
- Strip all asterisks and markdown formatting
- Remove all hyperlinks except at most 1–2 (keep most relevant)
- Check length: under 150 words or over 250? Good. 150–250? Rewrite to go shorter or longer.
- Scan for banned phrases — replace or delete
- Check opener and closer against banned lists
