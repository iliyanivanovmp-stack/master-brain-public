# Sales Call things

# Always must-have for a call

Show that you care about their situation. If they mention frustration or inefficiencies, acknowledge it: “I can see why that’s been stressful,”

**Be as calm as possible**

## Nick’s Sales Call Structure

### **1. Build Rapport (2–3 min)**

- Keep it light. The goal is to make them smile once.
- Example: a quick joke, small talk about their industry, or a compliment on recent work. - or simply ask where are you based? Really , no way! , I love this city
- Transition: *“Alright, you ready to dive in?”*

### **2. Set the Agenda (1–2 min)**

- Frame the call so they know exactly what’s coming.
- Script:
    
    “Here’s how I usually run these calls: first, I want to learn what made you take this call today. Then we’ll dig into your business and the challenges you’re running into. I’ll ask a few questions to get a sense of the size of those challenges — both in time and dollars. If I think I can help, I’ll walk you through what that looks like. At the end, you can decide if it makes sense to move forward. Sound good?”
    

This creates buy-in and sets up the “pain → investment” flow.

### **3. Discovery (10–15 min)**

- Start broad:
    - *“Why did you decide to book the call”*
    - How long has this problem  being going?
    - When do you need it fixed?
    - *“What’s your main goal for the next 3–6 months?”*
- Dig into pain:
    - *Why now? What’s changed that makes this urgent?”
    Why haven’t you done that yet?*
    - *“How have you tried to solve this before? What didn’t work?”*
- Deep-dive diagnostic:
    - *“Walk me through your customer journey, from first touch to fulfillment. Where are the biggest leaks?”*
    - *“What’s your current revenue per employee, or ROI per employee? Where do you feel that’s underperforming?”*
    - *“How much has the company grown over the last year? How far is that from where you want to be?”*
- Quantify the pain in $$$:
    - *“What does that bottleneck cost you right now — in lost revenue, wasted hours, or missed opportunities?”*
    - *“If this problem was solved, what would it be worth to you in monthly revenue or time saved?”*
    - Do you have a budget in mind that you see reasonable for fixing this problem?

### **4. Transition to Solution (2–3 min)**

- Acknowledge what they shared:
    
    “Thanks, that gives me a really clear picture. From what you’ve told me, the biggest pain is [X], which is costing you roughly [$].”
    
- Check alignment:
    
    “Is that accurate?”
    

This ensures you and the prospect agree on the size of the problem before you pitch.

### **5. Present the Solution (5–7 min)**

- Position your system as the best-fit fix.
- Script:
    
    “Here’s how I’d solve this. I’ve built similar systems for [industry/niche]. The approach is [describe your templated solution with slight customization]. This would eliminate [pain], save you [hours/$], and give you [desired outcome].”
    
    OR
    Okay , we’ve solved many similar to xyz before, to make a long story short ,we’re gonna solve X by doing Y , which will save you Z dollars each month.
    
- Keep checking in: *“Does this line up with what you were hoping for?”*
- Since this is our next point of discussion - have you thought about a logical budget to tackle this problem?

### **6. Frame the Investment (2–3 min)**

- Don’t use “cost” or “price.” Frame it against the pain.
- Script:

“Based on the scope, this project would be in the neighborhood of [$X]. Considering that [problem] is costing you [$Y per month], this isn’t really a cost — it’s an investment that pays for itself in [timeframe].”

BEST: Based on what you told me to save you that 50k$ , our project will cost around 10k$,

- Example: *“You told me missed leads are costing about $15K/month. The system I’m proposing is a one-time $5K investment. That means if it works as designed, it pays for itself in the first couple of weeks.”*

### **7. Next Steps (1–2 min)**

- Light close, no pressure.
- Script:
    
    “Here’s what happens next: I’ll scope this out in more detail and send you a proposal today. You can review it, and if everything looks good, we’ll get started right away. Sound like a plan?”
    
    ## Objection Handling
    
    [Objection Handling](Objection%20Handling%202bd8155828958052940bff13f59f76c8.md) 
    
    if they say too pricy - just offer to make it lower but to remove a feature of your project
    
    - Preempt most objections by qualifying up front.
    - If they hesitate:
        - *“What’s holding you back?”*
        - *“Is it the scope, the timeline, or the investment?”*
    - Tie it back to pain:
        - *“I get it. But if the problem is costing [$X] a month, does it make sense to keep waiting?”*
        - **Cost Concerns → Mention Potential ROI:**
            
            If they say it’s expensive, help them see the bigger picture. “I understand the cost is a concern. Consider that if we save you X hours per week and help close Y more deals, the return could more than cover the investment.”
            
        - **Doubts About AI → Share a Simple Success Story:**
            
            If they’re skeptical about AI’s effectiveness, give an example (without going into heavy details) of a similar business that benefited. “I recently worked with a consultant who used our AI strategy to streamline their outreach. They landed two new clients in the first month.”
            
        - **No Need for Complex Negotiation Tactics:**
            
            At this stage, just reassure them. “I hear your concern. Let’s make sure we’re focusing on results that matter to you.” Keep it calm and reinforcing the value.
            
        - **Confidence is Key:**
            
            If you seem nervous or unsure, they’ll pick up on that. Believe in your solution, trust your process, and communicate that you’re there to help them succeed.

## 1. The frame/the opening

**-  What you do:** time, agenda , outcome , permission to direct

**Clarify time: “** We have 30 minutes and I don’t want to hold you for more so let’s dive into it. Time still good?”

**Set the agenda:** ”I am gonna ask you a few questions to understand your current set-up , and what it’s costing you. If there is a real fit I will explain what i would recommend and what the next steps would be , if not , i’ll tell you that quickly. Sound fair?”

**Set outcome:**  :”By the end of this call we should know either one of two things - either there’s a clear problem worth solving and we map out the next step or we shake hands and we keep moving, deal?”

**Permission to lead:** Cool , let me start with this

**Whole example: “**Still good for 15?.  Perfect. I will ask a few questions to understand ,what’s happending and what’s costing you. If there is a real fit I will tell you exactly what i would recommend and we will decide the next steps , if not I will say that fast, sounds fair?

## 2. Diagnosis

**-  What you do:** find real problem under the surface symptoms

**Surface symptoms:** what’s happening
****What’s happening right now? | What made you took this call? | What would you like to be different?

 **Mechanism:** where/why it breaks
  - Walk me through what happens from start to finish. | Where does it break most often? | What part feels messy? | When it goes wrong what’s usually the cost?

**Impact** :what it costs
  What does this cost you in a normal week? If nothing changes how does this affect you? Your job: How does this affect revenue , time , risk, reputation ? What’s the hidden cost

**Attempts:** - what they tried
 - What have you tried so far? What have worked a little and what didn’t? Why you think it did not work? What are you worried will happen if you try again?

**Priority**:why it matters now
  - “Why is this important to fix now? | Where does this sit compared to other priorities? | What happens if we do nothing? | What would make this a clear win for you?”

## 3. Quantify

**-What you do:** convert the problem into cost,time,money,risk,reputation  - numbers

**Time** :”How many hours a week does this take ? , Who is doing it and what is this person supposed to be doing instead? If we gave that hours back what would change?”

**Money:**
L**eakage:** missed revenue
“How many leads come in per week? How many are missed cuz of this? What’s an average client worth?”
**Cost** - “What does this cost in staff time? What’s the cost of tools ? Cost of rework or mistake?”

**Risk**: one mistake can cost X
”What happens when this fails? Has it caused issues before? What’s the worst case if this happens again?”

**Reputation:** negative thoughts about their brand 
”How fast do you respond now? Do you lose clients because of this? Any complains or negative reviews tied to this? If a competitor responds in 5 minutes what happens?”

**Wrap-up:**
”So that is costing you X every month
Is that fair or i am missing something? “

## 5. Decision

**What you do:** Diagnose the decision making process and push it with questions

If nothing changes you keep paying the cost every month. The fix is simply removing the bottleneck , no hype, just cause and effect.

Who is the economic buyer - if we decide to move who has final authority on this

Who else would have weight on this , your team , someone else who is not in this call

What’s the normal process for approving something like this , budget approval , or sign off. |What would need to be true to convert this quickly.

What usually stops things like this going ahead.

## 8.Objection Prevention

**What you do:** you surface most objections early and kill them so they don’t float later on

”If we were to move forward, what would you need to see and believe for it to be an obvious yes?”
”What could stop this from happening even if it makes sense to both of us? “

”Is there anything you are unsure about, even though outcomes are attractive? “

”For this to be an obvious yes, what would you need to say? “

”What could stop this even if it makes sense. “
”Is there anything that you are unsure about that would make you hesitate? “

## 11. Proposals

**What you do:** don’t send a pitch deck , but confirmation document

**Content of the proposal:**
The problem you confirmed, or the cost you quantified, the outcome you agreed,the path you chose, maybe the constraints you respected, and the next step you booked. 

**No proposal without a calendar**. (Optional)- Absolutely, I'll send it. Let's book 15 minutes now to review it so it doesn't end up sitting in your inbox.  Two slots tomorrow, 11 a.m. or 3 p.m. If 

**Proposal Title**: "reduce lead response time by sub 5 minutes in 14 days", as an example, or "stop missed follow-ups and recover lost bookings". If the title is outcome-based, the brain reads value first.

- **Make sure you always put diagnosis on one page. The first section should be:**

This makes it feel like the proposal is bespoke and already anchored in their reality.
    - what they told you
    - what the current state is
    - the breakdown point
    - the quantified cost
    - why now?
- **Clear scope box** with inclusions and exclusions and time frame with checkpoints.
- **Decision Criteria.(optional)** This makes sense if you care about X and what Y by Z.  it's designed to be a walk through in 10 to 15 minutes so we can align and decide, and then we're still on four".
And then mention the date and the time that you booked on the decision call.

    - an outcome

    - some cost
    - a proposed next step
    - some type of scope
    - a timeline

## 12. Post call follow-through

**What you do:** Outcome + Next steps

- Reduce missed follow-ups
- Booked Tuesday 11 a.m.

The body should be in this order:

1. The decision context (one sentence as to why they took the call)
2. The real problem (the diagnosis) and please no big paragraphs; you can use bullet points, but try to keep everything short and concise
3. The cost quantified (time, money, risk, reputation) and you got to include at least one number here
4. The desired outcome, usually time bound (before and after and the time frame)
5. The constraint or the decision criteria (what must be true for a yes)
6. The next step (you're telling them exactly what it is and what it produces)
7. Ownership (you versus them, what you or what they owe)
8. A calendar confirmation (the day and time and who's attending)
