# Business Research

# Phase 1 (B2B) — The Research Phase for Businesses

## Lesson 1 — ICB (Ideal Customer Business)

*Goal: define the **firm** you want, not the person you want.*

**A. Firmographics & economics**

- Industry/segment? Region(s)? Revenue band? Employee count? Growth rate?
- Business model & unit economics (gross margin, ACV/AOV, sales cycle length)?
- Go-to-market motion: PLG, sales-led, channel, marketplace?
- Buying cycle timing: fiscal year, budget season, renewal months?

**B. Technographics & process landscape**

- Current tools/stack for the job you impact? Integrations required?
- Data sources and quality (where does truth live)?
- IT/security posture (SSO, SOC2/ISO needs, data residency, procurement gates)?

**C. Buying committee (map roles, not just titles)**

- Economic buyer, technical buyer, legal/procurement, security/compliance, admins, power users, champion.
- How many stakeholders typically weigh in? (Expect many; plan consensus.) [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/buyer-enablement?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**D. Triggers & timing**

- External: funding, M&A, new regulation, market shocks.
- Internal: new execs, KPIs missed, tech replacement cycles, hiring sprees/job posts.
- Peer/third-party influence: review sites, analyst notes, communities. (Peer reviews materially shape B2B buying. ) [G2 Research Hub+2G2 Research Hub+2](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**E. Job-to-be-Done (for the business)**

- What job is the **business** “hiring” a solution to accomplish (functional, emotional, social outcomes)? [Christensen Institute+2Harvard Business Review+2](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Lesson 2 — Current State (Business)

*Goal: diagnose operating pain in the company’s words & numbers.*

**Question bank (pick many):**

- What KPI is stuck? (Revenue, CAC/LTV, churn, NRR/GRR, cycle time, backlog, SLA breaches.)
- Where are costs ballooning? (FTE hours, rework, vendor bloat, “shadow IT”.)
- Which processes fail? (handoffs, approvals, integrations, data hygiene, governance.)
- What have they already tried? Why did those attempts fail?
- Where is risk highest? (security, compliance, downtime, vendor lock-in.)
- What blockers slow deals/shipments? (legal redlines, IT queues, data access.)
- What are frontline complaints vs. exec complaints? (collect both.)
- Internal language: how do *they* describe the pain in reviews/forums/town halls? (Pull verbatims from G2/Capterra/communities to mirror their words.) [G2 Research Hub+1](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- What happens if nothing changes? (Cost of delay; quantify.)

*(Remember: B2B buyers navigate omnichannel content and want rep-optional paths—your research should assume they self-educate heavily.)* [Harvard Business Review+1](https://hbr.org/2022/02/traditional-b2b-sales-and-marketing-are-becoming-obsolete?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## Lesson 3 — Dream State (Business Outcomes)

*Goal: paint the “after” in measurable business terms.*

**Question bank:**

- If they could fix this perfectly, which **3 KPIs** move, and by how much?
- What becomes *possible* operationally? (Lead time ↓, cycle time ↓, win-rate ↑.)
- Financial picture: payback period, ROI horizon, budget source.
- Team outcomes: fewer escalations, happier ICs/managers, lower turnover.
- Strategic lift: faster expansion, new markets, compliance readiness, valuation story.
- Buying-committee wins: how does each stakeholder personally “win” (CFO, CTO, Ops, end users)?
- What would peers notice first? (Social proof they care about.)
- What must be true for this to be credible in *their* context?

## Lesson 4 — Roadblocks (Org & Process Constraints)

*Goal: list many, isolate the **key** organizational bottleneck.*

**Common roadblocks to check:**

- **Consensus risk:** too many stakeholders, misaligned criteria, “no decision.” (Plan to *enable* consensus.) [Gartner+1](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/buyer-enablement?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Process debt:** no owner, unclear RACI, approval spaghetti.
- **IT backlog & security:** integration effort, data posture, infosec review gates.
- **Data quality:** duplicate records, missing fields, schema sprawl.
- **Change management:** training, habits, incentives, “not invented here.”
- **Value proof gap:** no agreed baseline, weak success criteria.

**Prioritization lenses you can use (pick one):**

- **MEDDICC** (Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria/process, Identify pain, Champion, Competition). [Meddicc+1](https://meddicc.com/meddpicc-sales-methodology-and-process?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **SPICED** (Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision). [Winning by Design+1](https://winningbydesign.com/spiced-framework/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **BANT** check (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline) as a sanity pass, not the whole story. [AmolinoAI+1](https://amolino.ai/resources/frameworks/qualification/bant?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Pick the KEY roadblock:**

- Which single constraint—if removed—unlocks 80% of progress?
- What lightweight test would validate that’s the bottleneck (pilot, sandbox, proof-of-value)?

## Lesson 5 — Solution (Business “must do”)

*Goal: prescribe the **business action/system** that removes the key roadblock.*

**Question bank:**

- What must they *implement* (process change, governance, integration, data cleanup, training)?
- How will they align the buying committee (mutually agreed success criteria, decision memo)?
- What is the simplest pilot/P.O.V. to prove value fast (data slice, single team, one integration)?
- What is the **If–Then** bridge: **“If they <implement X> then they will <hit Y outcome>.”**
- Who owns rollout, enablement, and measurement? What cadence keeps it alive?

---

## Lesson 6 — Product (map your offer to the Solution)

*Goal: position the product as the **fastest, safest vehicle** to the Dream State.*

**Question bank:**

- How exactly does the product **implement the Solution** (features → workflows)?
- How does it **increase the odds of success** (templates, services, CS playbooks, governance)?
- How does it get results **faster** (time-to-value, integrations, automation)?
- How does it require **less effort/sacrifice** (change mgmt, migration tools, SSO, admin UX)?
- What do businesses **like/hate** in alternatives (pull verbatims from peer reviews/analysts to shape differentiators)? [G2 Research Hub+1](https://research.g2.com/hubfs/2024-buyer-behavior-report.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- What proof will *this* committee trust (logos, case studies, ROI model, security docs)?
- What is your risk reversal (pilot terms, opt-outs, payback guarantee)?

# Phase 2 (B2B) — The Writing Phase (fast mapping)

Use your existing frameworks, tuned to businesses:

- **DIC**:
    - **Disrupt:** “The metric your CFO cares about is stuck—and here’s why effort won’t move it.”
    - **Intrigue:** tease the mechanism and consensus plan; stack *business* fascinations.
    - **Click:** “See the 14-day pilot plan and KPI model.”
- **PAS**:
    - **Pain:** stalled KPI + cost of delay; **Amplify:** status with board/CFO, future-pacing next quarter; **Solution:** pilot + success criteria, urgency via budget cycle.
- **HSO (first-person)**:
    - Hook at max drama (missed quarter, lost deal); Story (failed attempts, consensus mess → glimpse of new operating rhythm); Offer (pilot brief / ROI worksheet).
- **Opt-in page**: headline ties to board-level KPI; body uses fascinations (compliance, time-to-value, integration checklist); bait = **pilot pack** (success criteria template, timeline, risk log).
- **Email sequence**:
    1. Welcome + deliver pilot pack.
    2. HSO story (belief shift: from “tool” to “operating mechanism”).
    3. DIC value (3 roadblocks that kill deployments + fixes).
    4. DIC sales (mechanism → offer).
    5. PAS sell (cost of delay; real deadline: procurement window/quarter close).
- **Long-form letter (10 parts)**: same structure, but every benefit is a **business KPI** and every proof point is **committee-friendly** (security, ROI, case studies, references).

### Two closing notes (so you win B2B more often)

1. **Design for consensus** (not a single persona). Equip your champion with a short **decision memo** that answers: *Why now, why us, why this budget, why safe?* (B2B buyers struggle to reach agreement; help them do it.)
